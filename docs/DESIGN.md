# Design choices

## Why three small workflows

Each addresses a different failure point: starting before the environment is ready, trusting one persuasive answer, and losing the reasoning at a handoff. They can be used independently. A small task should not require the whole set.

## Evidence over ceremony

Checkpoint records what was checked and what remains unknown. Mise en Place asks for a source or observable result behind readiness. Neither creates truth by formatting it into a table.

## Separate opinions, then a decision

Megazord gathers independent reviews before synthesis. The decision record should preserve disagreement and uncertainty instead of treating model agreement as proof. A person remains accountable for consequential choices.

## Instructions versus enforcement

Checkpoint and Mise en Place are operating procedures. Megazord includes executable controls whose behavior is tested separately. Documentation does not imply that every instruction is enforced by code, or that CLI settings are a complete security boundary.

## Bounded work and truthful status

A useful result may be partial or blocked. Run limits and explicit status make that visible. Retrying indefinitely, hiding a failed reviewer, or presenting a fixture as a live result would defeat the purpose.

## Portability and privacy

The package removes machine-specific paths, private source material, and retired project assumptions. Examples share a fictional equipment-checkout scenario. Live users still need to review prompts, provider terms, credentials, outputs, and publication permissions for their own context.
